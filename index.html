What this actually costs you

A canonical tag is a page saying “I am not the original, rank that one instead.” It exists to stop duplicate pages competing with each other. Here it is set to the homepage on every single page, which means your entire site has volunteered to be ignored except the front door.

So when somebody in Cincinnati searches for commercial solar, your commercial page cannot answer. It has told Google it is a copy of a page about premium residential solar. Same for EV charging, battery storage, and every case study you have published.

This is a template fault, not something you did. It is one line in whoever built the site, and fixing it is a single change that applies to all fourteen pages at once. It is the highest-value hour of work available on this site by a wide margin.

Half your sitemap is broken links. Three of the six URLs return 404. That is a trust signal going the wrong way every time Google reads it.

But the real cost is what is missing. Every page that would win you commercial work — the commercial service page, the four service pages, the portfolio and its five case studies — is absent from the map you hand to Google. Combined with Finding 1, those pages are both unlisted and self-suppressed.

Finding 3 — You Told Us You Want Roofer Work. The Site Has Not One Word About It.
HOMEPAGE WORD COUNTS found commercial 4 residential 4 battery 4 kentucky 3 ohio 3 roofer 0 roofing 0 contractor 0 partner / trade / referral 0 PAGES AIMED AT A ROOFING COMPANY none exist
This is the gap worth closing first, because it is the one you already decided on

You said you want to do work for roofers and build relationships with roofing companies. A roofing contractor who needs a solar partner has nowhere to land on your site and nothing to read that speaks to them. Everything on it is written for a homeowner.

A roofer is not a lead, they are a channel. One roofing company that sends you work sends it repeatedly, which is a different economic shape from winning single homeowners one at a time. That matters more than usual when you are two installers and the retail market is quiet.

What it needs is a page written to them, not to a homeowner: what you take off their plate, how you handle the electrical side, how quickly you can be on site, and what the arrangement looks like. It costs nothing but the writing, and it is the only page on the site with a clear commercial job to do.

Small field, local consequence

You are publishing a business with a street address, no town and no state. Structured data is how Google reads your location without guessing from your words, and this tells it almost nothing usable.

Everything else in that block is right — phone, service list, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok all correctly declared. It is three fields, and it is a five-minute fix.

The evidence a commercial buyer wants is already on your site, just not arranged for them

A distillery, a facilities company and a farm are exactly the references a small commercial buyer asks for. They are sitting in a general portfolio at roughly 75 words each, undated, with no system size, no load, no timescale and no outcome.

Three properly written commercial case studies would do more for you than any amount of new content. What the site was, what you installed, how long it took, what it saved. That is what gets forwarded internally when somebody is deciding whether to call you.

Why this matters to how we would price it

You are not starting from nothing, and you should not be sold as if you were. The build is sound and the content exists. What is broken is configuration and targeting, which is cheap to fix and fast to show.

Findings 1, 2 and 4 are a single afternoon. Finding 3 is writing. Finding 5 is writing. There is no rebuild in this list.

Priority Roadmap
  • Fix the canonical tags. One template change, thirteen pages stop suppressing themselves. Nothing else on this list works until this does.
  • Rebuild the sitemap so it lists the pages that exist and drops the three that do not.
  • Fill in the three address fields in the structured data.
  • Write the roofing-partner page. The channel you already decided you want, which currently has no front door.
  • Rewrite the three commercial case studies properly — system size, timescale, outcome.
  • Then, and only then, consider anything paid. There is no sense buying traffic for pages that are telling Google to ignore them.

The build is fine. The configuration is throwing away thirteen of them.

Fix that first, and everything you have already paid to have built starts working.
